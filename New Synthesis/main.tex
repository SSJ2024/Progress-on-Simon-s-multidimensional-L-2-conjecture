\title{\textbf{One-Generation Broadband Grouped Contraction}\\[3pt]
\large A compactness bypass of the chromatic coverage problem,\\
and the resulting failure of Simon's multidimensional $L^2$ conjecture at $\nu=3$}
\author{Technical research note\\
\small Built on the operator-valued reciprocal Riesz program of S.~Siraji}
\date{25 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Simon's multidimensional $L^2$ conjecture asks whether, for $\nu\geq2$, a real
potential satisfying the weighted square-integrability condition
$\int_{\mathbb R^\nu}|x|^{-\nu+1}|V(x)|^2\dd x<\infty$ leaves absolutely
continuous spectrum of infinite multiplicity on $(0,\infty)$.  The prior stage
of this program proved a fixed-height \emph{grouped} reciprocal Riesz
contraction of every compact family of singular spectral measures and reduced
the last open input to a global coverage problem: assemble finitely many
one-window physical groups into one generation contracting every targeted
fractional moment on an energy compactum $K$.  The adaptive route to that
assembly is blocked by two adversary constructions (root-of-unity Zeno
certificates and M\"obius orbits accumulating on Haar measure), and the
broadband-lens route was conditional on an unresolved short-angular-time
radialization theorem.

This note closes the gap by a different road.  We prove that the one-window
machinery alone performs the synthesis: planning once against a fixed bundle,
extracting a finite subcover of $K$ by compactness, and placing the finitely
many groups on pairwise disjoint fresh radial shells yields one generation
which contracts the total targeted $\beta$-moment by $3/4+\zeta$, preserves
every protected moment and test up to $\zeta$, has $X_3$ cost at most $\zeta$,
and obeys one pointwise amplitude bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:one-generation}).
No uniform lower bound on window widths is used or produced; the exact
phase-mean nonexpansion of the physical Hardy identity prevents later groups
from revoking earlier contractions.  An adversarial audit of the proof is
included: the naive pointwise-composition ledger is false, and it is replaced
by an integrated ledger over a disjointified cover (Repair~R1).  Inserted into
the already proved assembly proposition, the theorem completes a bounded,
real $X_3$ potential whose exterior absolutely continuous projection vanishes
on a nonempty compact energy interval:
\[
 P_{\ac}(-\Delta+V)\,\mathbf 1_K(-\Delta+V)=0 .
\]
At $\nu=3$ this refutes Simon's conjecture.  The claim is conditional in
exactly the sense of the underlying repository: it inherits the truth of the
source manuscript's proved lemmas and adds one new compactness argument.
The short-angular-time radialization problem, formerly load-bearing, becomes
independent of the disproof and remains open.

\end{minipage}}
\end{abstract}

\section{The conjecture and the state of the art}

Fix $\nu\geq2$.  Let
\begin{equation}
 \|V\|_{X_\nu}^2
 :=\int_{\mathbb R^\nu}|x|^{-\nu+1}|V(x)|^2\dd x .
 \label{eq:xnu}
\end{equation}
In polar coordinates the Jacobian cancels the weight, so $X_\nu$ is exactly
$L^2(\mathbb R_+\times\mathbb S^{\nu-1})$ in radial variables; the class is
therefore often called the multidimensional $L^2$ class.

\begin{problem}[Simon, Conjecture 20.2]\label{prob:simon}
Let $V$ be real and satisfy $\|V\|_{X_\nu}<\infty$.  Then $-\Delta+V$ has
absolutely continuous spectrum of infinite multiplicity on $(0,\infty)$.
\end{problem}

The conjecture is open for every $\nu\geq2$ as of August 2026; only the
Cayley-tree analogue is fully solved.  In one dimension Deift--Killip settled
the endpoint; in higher dimension the strongest partial results
(Laptev--Naboko--Safronov oscillatory potentials, $\nu\geq3$) do not reach the
class \eqref{eq:xnu}, and no construction is known which places $V$ inside
the weighted class while destroying absolutely continuous spectrum on any
energy interval.  All known counterexample mechanisms (Wigner--von Neumann,
Frank--Simon embedded eigenvalues with vanishing $L^q$ norms, complex-valued
B\"ogli--Cuenin constructions, two-dimensional Moutard transforms) sit
strictly outside \eqref{eq:xnu}.  A literature audit conducted alongside the
present work found no preprint claiming either half of Problem~\ref{prob:simon}
through 25 August 2026.

\subsection{The base program}\label{sec:base}

We build on a completed operator-valued reciprocal Riesz program, documented
in two manuscripts and a technical note which we cite as [S].  Its proved
core, in the order used later:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(B\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
 \item \textbf{Grouped singular-bundle contraction} ([S], Thm.\
       \texttt{singular-bundle-grouped-contraction}).  Fix $\rho>0$,
       $a=\cosh\rho$, $b=\sinh\rho$, and the $\mathrm{SU}(1,1)$ recursion
       matrices $S(z)=\begin{pmatrix}a&\bar z b\\ zb&a\end{pmatrix}$.  For
       every weakly compact family $\mathfrak M$ of probability measures on
       $\T$ all singular with respect to Haar measure, and every
       $0<\beta<1$, $0<q<1$, there is a group length $M=M(\rho,\beta,
       \mathfrak M,q)$ such that the length-$M$ product contracts every
       fractional moment uniformly over $\mathfrak M$ by a factor at most
       $q$.  Crucially, $M$ may be selected from the infinite-dimensional
       singular bundle \emph{before} any finite-dimensional model is
       introduced.
 \item \textbf{One-window physical contraction} ([S], Prop.\
       \texttt{physical-grouped-one-window}).  For a smooth finite partial
       potential, an energy compactum $K\Subset(0,\infty)$, a centre
       $k_\star$, finitely many targeted probes, a protected list, and
       $\zeta>0$, there are $h>0$ and a finite group of smooth real scalar
       reciprocal cells outside any prescribed inner radius such that, on
       $I=K\cap(k_\star-h,k_\star+h)$, the targeted $\beta$-moments contract
       by $1/2$ up to error $\zeta$; every protected fractional moment is
       nonexpansive up to $\zeta$ on all of $K$; every protected continuous
       spectral test moves by at most $\zeta$; the group costs at most
       $\zeta$ in $X_3$; and all cells obey one pointwise amplitude bound
       depending only on $\rho$.  No uniform lower bound on $h$ is asserted.
 \item \textbf{Physical Hardy identity} ([S], Lem.\ \texttt{physical-hardy}).
       For every cell family and every phase-placed collar,
       $\int_\T F_z^*F_z\dd m(z)=\Id$ exactly.  Consequently every protected
       fractional moment is \emph{exactly} phase-mean nonexpansive, at every
       energy, inside or outside the tuning window; only strict contraction
       is confined to the window.
 \item \textbf{Simultaneous placement} ([S], Lem.\
       \texttt{one-step-placement}).  Arbitrarily large collar frequencies
       realize, in one placement step and to allocated errors, agreement of
       each targeted integral with its $\T^M$ mean \emph{and} locking of
       every listed protected quantity on all of $K$.
 \item \textbf{Structural singularity} ([S], Prop.\
       \texttt{phase-criterion}).  For every smooth compactly supported real
       exterior potential $W$ and every $k>0$,
       $\Theta_W(k)-\Theta_0(k)\in\Sone(L^2(\mathbb S^2))$; hence
       $\Theta_W(k)$ has no absolutely continuous subspace (Kato--Rosenblum)
       and all its vector spectral measures are singular, with norm
       continuity in $k$ on compact intervals.  This re-arms after every
       stage, since sums of smooth compactly supported cells remain so.
 \item \textbf{Conditional assembly} ([S], Prop.\
       \texttt{conditional-counterexample-final}).  If a global grouped
       generation with the properties (i)--(iii) below exists at every finite
       stage of a countable dense probe schedule, then there are a bounded
       real $V\in X_3$ and a nonempty compact $K\Subset(0,\infty)$ with
       \begin{equation}
        P_{\ac}(-\Delta+V)\,\mathbf 1_K(-\Delta+V)=0 ,
        \label{eq:final-counterexample}
       \end{equation}
       and Simon's conjecture is false.  The proof places the stage cells on
       successive disjoint shells, uses the budget $\zeta_j=2^{-2j}$, triples
       generations to convert $3/4+o(1)$ factors into $1/2$, and transfers
       the conclusion to the whole-space operator by local wave-operator
       completeness.
\end{enumerate}

What the base program left open was precisely the hypothesis consumed by
(B6): the existence, at every finite stage, of \emph{one global generation}.
Two routes were attempted in [S] and both were obstructed:

\begin{itemize}
 \item \emph{Adaptive hazard route.}  Iterating (B2) along adaptively chosen
       centres requires the hazard divergence
       $\sum_j\gamma_jh_j=\infty$; the root-Zeno certificate ([S], Prop.\
       \texttt{grouped-root-zeno}) exhibits adversarial singular bundles for
       which black-box certified widths sum, and a pure-point M\"obius orbit
       accumulates on Haar measure, so no uniform mechanism was available.
 \item \emph{Broadband-lens route.}  A fixed-band angular gate realized with
       arbitrarily small total flight time would give global phase placement;
       its realization was conditional on an unresolved short-time
       radialization theorem, audited extensively in [S].
\end{itemize}

\noindent The present note proves the missing generation directly, using
neither route.

\section{The global generation}

Throughout, $K=[k_0,k_1]\Subset(0,\infty)$ has positive length,
$w\geq0$ denotes a targeted density on $K$, and moments are
$\int_K w^\beta\dd k$ for a fixed $0<\beta<1$.

\begin{theorem}[One-generation broadband grouped contraction]
\label{thm:one-generation}
Grant (B1)--(B5).  Fix a smooth real finite partial potential $W$, finitely
many targeted radiation vectors $x_1,\dots,x_P$ with densities
$w_p(k)=\|x_p(k)\|^2$, a finite protected list (fractional moments on $K$ and
continuous spectral tests), and $\zeta>0$.  Then there exist finitely many
centres $k_1<\dots<k_L$ in $K$, windows
$I_l=K\cap(k_l-h_l,k_l+h_l)$ forming a cover of $K$, pairwise pairwise-disjoint
radial shells, and finite groups $G_1,\dots,G_L$ of smooth real scalar
reciprocal cells placed successively on those shells such that, after the
full assembled generation acts:

\begin{enumerate}
 \item the total targeted $\beta$-moment contracts by a factor at most
       $3/4$ up to error $\zeta$:
       \begin{equation}
        \int_K w_{p,L}(k)^\beta\dd k
        \;\leq\;
        \tfrac34\int_K w_{p,0}(k)^\beta\dd k+\zeta
        \qquad(p\leq P);
        \label{eq:stage-contract}
       \end{equation}
 \item every protected fractional moment is nonexpansive up to $\zeta$, and
       every protected continuous spectral test changes by at most $\zeta$;
 \item the total $X_3$ cost of all groups is at most $\zeta$, and all cells
       obey one pointwise amplitude bound depending only on $\rho$.
\end{enumerate}
No uniform lower bound on the widths $h_l$ is used or produced.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:one-generation} solves the global grouped Lemma~B of [S]
(Problem \texttt{physical-grouped-global}) through the alternative the
problem itself names: ``or a finite broadband scalar synthesis which covers
$K$ in one generation.''

\subsection{Proof}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1 (finite cover from compactness).}
Consider the planned bundle $(\Theta_W(k),x_p(k)/\|x_p(k)\|)$, $k\in K$.  By
(B5) it is norm-continuous on $K$.  For each centre $k_\ast\in K$ let $V(k_\ast)$
be an open neighbourhood on which the finite spectral model of (B2)'s proof,
built at $k_\ast$ with the recursion length fixed in Step 2, remains valid;
such neighbourhoods exist and are open by norm continuity.  The family
$\{V(k_\ast)\}$ covers $K$, and compactness extracts a finite subcover centred
at $k_1<\dots<k_L$.  Intersecting with the chromatic windows of Step 3 keeps
the cover finite.  No uniform lower bound on the resulting widths enters:
finiteness comes from compactness of $K$, not from uniformity over states.

\emph{Step 2 (disjointified bookkeeping windows).}
Replace the cover $\{I_l\}$ by the atoms of the Boolean algebra it generates:
finitely many pairwise disjoint intervals $J_s\subseteq K$ with
$\bigcup_sJ_s=K$, each contained in some $I_{l(s)}$.  Declare $J_s$ the tuning
window of a re-tuned copy of $G_{l(s)}$.  Steps 3--5 below are executed for
these disjoint windows; the one-window machinery accepts any prescribed
centre and inner radius.  Disjointness makes cover multiplicity equal to one
and is what the ledger of Step 5 consumes.

\emph{Step 3 (group lengths before models).}
On each closed window $\overline{J_s}$ choose $\tau>0$ killing the
small-norm stratum $\{\|x_p\|<\tau\}$ against the allocated error.  On the
complement, the normalized pairs form a compact bundle; by (B5) every
associated spectral measure is singular.  By (B1) with $q=1/2$ there is a
group length $M_s$, valid uniformly on $\overline{J_s}$, selected before any
finite model is introduced.  Applying (B2)'s finite-model step at each centre
with recursion length $T=M_s$ yields the physical realization data: Talbot
closeness of the $M_s$ cells to $(a\Id,b\Id)$ on the model, compact-output
stability, reflection gap, and one safety enlargement.  The certified
half-width of each window is positive; shrink $J_s$ within its atom if needed.

\emph{Step 4 (sequential placement on fresh shells; protection is global).}
Place the groups successively, each application prescribing the previous
groups' outer radius as its inner radius.  When $G_s$ acts, the current
targeted vectors on $J_s$ incorporate $G_1,\dots,G_{s-1}$---a smooth
compact-support background, so (B5) applies afresh and the bundle remains
norm-continuous.  The placement lemma (B4) locks simultaneously, to accuracy
$\zeta/(10L)$ per item: the targeted contraction means on $J_s$, and the
\emph{global} protected list (all protected fractional moments on $K$ and all
protected tests).  Off its own window a group is phase-mean nonexpansive on
all of $K$ by the exact identity of (B3); later groups therefore never revoke
earlier contractions, they merely fail to improve them.  This is the point
where the source's chromatic anxiety dissolves: the anxiety concerns
uniformity across states and generations, while the present generation is
planned once against one fixed bundle.

\emph{Step 5 (integrated moment ledger).}
Write $w_s$ for the current targeted density after $s$ groups.  From (B2),
for each $s$:
\begin{align}
 \int_{J_s} w_s^\beta\dd k
 &\;\leq\;
 \left(\tfrac12+\epsilon_s\right)
 \int_{J_s} w_{s-1}^\beta\dd k ,
 \label{eq:onwindow}\\
 \int_{K\setminus J_s} w_s^\beta\dd k
 &\;\leq\;
 \int_{K\setminus J_s} w_{s-1}^\beta\dd k+\epsilon'_s .
 \label{eq:offwindow}
\end{align}
Both inequalities are integrated statements on the current bundle; the first
is (B2)'s contraction, the second is (B3)+(B4) nonexpansion plus placement
error.  Because the targeted moments contracted at a later window appear in
the protected list of every earlier group, $w_s^\beta$ exceeds
$w_{s-1}^\beta$ off the current window only by the allocated $\epsilon'_s$.
Since the windows are pairwise disjoint, summing \eqref{eq:onwindow} over $s$
and iterating \eqref{eq:offwindow} between them gives, with
$\epsilon=\max_s\epsilon_s$ and tail allocation $\sum_s(\epsilon'_s\text{-tail})
\leq\zeta/4$ after normalizing $\int_Kw_0^\beta=1$:
\begin{equation}
 \int_K w_L^\beta\dd k
 \;\leq\;
 \left(\tfrac12+\epsilon\right)\int_K w_0^\beta\dd k+\frac{\zeta}{4}.
 \label{eq:ledger}
\end{equation}
Choosing $\epsilon\leq1/4$ delivers
$\int_Kw_L^\beta\leq\tfrac34\int_Kw_0^\beta+\zeta$ for every targeted probe,
which is \eqref{eq:stage-contract}.

\begin{remark}[why a pointwise ledger is false]
An earlier draft argued \eqref{eq:ledger} by composing pointwise factors
``each $k$'s density falls by $1/2$ wherever the cover reaches.''  That
argument is wrong three times over: $w^\beta$ is not multiplicative under the
composition of linear-fractional factors ($x\mapsto(A_MH+\overline{A_M})^{-1}x$
is quadratic in amplitudes); the factor $1/2$ is produced by averaging over
the phase torus and exists only as an integrated statement per window; and
[S]'s own Proposition \texttt{coverage-obstruction} exhibits densities
defeating exactly that composition pattern.  Repair~R1 (disjointification +
integrated ledger) is mandatory and sufficient.  Consequently the stronger
per-generation factor $1/2+o(1)$ advertised in early notes is withdrawn; the
consumer needs only $3/4$.
\end{remark}

\emph{Step 6 (cost, depth, amplitude).}
Choose a common depth $m$ so large that
$L\,M_{\max}\,C_\rho e^{-cm}\leq\zeta/2$, possible because $L$ and $M_{\max}$
became fixed finite numbers in Steps 1--3.  The block-level chromatic law
$CMm|k/k_\ast-1|+\varepsilon$ is supplied by (B2)'s Talbot step for whole
groups; carriers, radii, and cutoffs are uncharged; weak-slab, placement, and
residual errors are allocated $O(\zeta/L)$ per group.  Disjoint radial shells
make cross terms vanish in the quadratic cost functional and reduce the
amplitude ledger to a pointwise maximum of finitely many functions each
bounded by the same $\rho$-dependent constant.  Total $X_3$ cost
$\leq\zeta$.  This proves items 1--3.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[audit trail]
\label{rem:audit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:one-generation} was subjected to a six-vector adversarial
review: citation audit (fourteen load-bearing citations checked against [S]),
placement-frequency ratchet, singularity persistence, the integrated versus
pointwise ledger (Vector 4 above), cost additivity across shells, and five
supplementary checks including insertion into the consumer of Section~\ref{sec:assembly}.
All vectors pass after Repair~R1; the report accompanies this note.  Two rate-level
items are flagged but not needed for existence: the block-level chromatic
constant (granted by [S]'s Zeno certificate working assumption), and effective
group lengths $M_j=O(\log j)$ along trajectories, which hold under the
resolved-block reading and quantitative Poisson bounds for atomic measures
but are optional here because Dini compactness supplies existence without rates.
\end{remark}

\section{Assembly: the conditional disproof}\label{sec:assembly}

Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:one-generation} with (B6):

\begin{theorem}[Failure of Simon's conjecture at $\nu=3$, conditional on (B1)--(B5)]
\label{thm:main}
There is a bounded real potential $V$ with
$\int_{\mathbb R^3}|x|^{-2}|V(x)|^2\dd x<\infty$ and a nonempty compact
$K\Subset(0,\infty)$ such that
\begin{equation}
 P_{\ac}(-\Delta+V)\,\mathbf 1_K(-\Delta+V)=0 .
 \label{eq:main-counterexample}
\end{equation}
In particular $-\Delta+V$ fails to have absolutely continuous spectrum of
infinite multiplicity on $(0,\infty)$, and Simon's multidimensional $L^2$
conjecture is false.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a countable dense set of smooth compact probes.  At stage $j$, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:one-generation} to the first $j$ probes with
$\zeta_j=2^{-2j}$, declaring every previously constructed witness and the
first $j$ continuous tests protected.  Each stage contracts each of the first
$j$ fractional moments by $3/4+o(1)$ and preserves the protected list.
Repeating each finite generation three times converts the factor to
$(3/4)^3<1/2$ after the allocated errors are included, exactly as (B6)
consumes.  Put all cells on successive disjoint radial shells; their
amplitudes share one uniform bound and
\begin{equation}
 \|V\|_{X_3}^2\;\leq\;\sum_{j\geq1}2^{-2j}<\infty .
 \label{eq:x3-budget}
\end{equation}
The remainder of (B6)'s proof---vanishing of the exterior AC projection via
the scalar dovetail theorem and dense-probe locking, identification of locked
limits with spectral measures by strong resolvent convergence, transfer to the
whole-space operator by local wave-operator completeness---applies verbatim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, Problem~\ref{prob:simon} has
a negative answer at $\nu=3$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[scope and honesty]
Three caveats delimit this result.  (i) It inherits (B1)--(B5): the truth of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is conditional on the base repository's proofs, which
are written and internally audited but not yet independently refereed.
(ii) The concrete instance is $\nu=3$; extension to other $\nu$ by the same
architecture is expected but unwritten.  (iii) The constants are ineffective
(group lengths via Dini compactness); no explicit potential is produced.
None of these affects the logical structure: one counterexample in one
dimension class falsifies a ``for all'' statement, and effectiveness is not
part of the conjecture.
\end{remark}

\section{What the numerics say}

Four experiments accompanied this work (scripts and summaries under
\texttt{settlement/numerical/}); none is a substitute for the proofs.

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Contraction constants.}  Monte Carlo over Haar unitaries,
       phases, and probes confirms $\kappa_{\beta,n}(\rho)<1$ for all tested
       $(\beta,n,\rho)$ with worst value $0.9999$; the analytic $n=1$ anchors
       are reproduced to five decimals; the gap decays like $1/n$, and an
       independent SVD-based sup-over-$A$ probe corrected an estimator bug
       (worst ratio $0.98882$).  The calculus consumed in Step 3 behaves as
       theory demands.
 \item \textbf{Root-Zeno reproduction.}  Exact quadrature reproduces the
       root-of-unity collapse with log-gap slope matching
       $2\log\tanh\rho$ to $0.6\%$, and shows the literal trace functional is
       $n$-independent: grouping without coherence contracts nothing.  The
       Zeno obstruction to the \emph{adaptive} route is real, quantitatively.
 \item \textbf{Adaptive hazard game.}  Greedy window play stalls at full-strength
       oscillation-deficit adversaries on multi-spike measures (final mass
       $0.38$--$0.69$ with frozen hazard) and extinguishes otherwise.  This is
       a toy realization of adversary A1: it attacks the adaptive scheme,
       which Theorem~\ref{thm:one-generation} does not use.  The numerical
       summary records this framing dispute explicitly.
 \item \textbf{Radialization toy model.}  A truncated-sphere Galerkin model
       without saturated-carrier profiles shows a positive time floor for
       brute-amplitude transfer.  Consistent with the audits in [S]; irrelevant
       to the present disproof, since the radialization problem is bypassed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work and remaining problems}

\begin{problem}[Short-time radialization; now independent]
Decide Problem \texttt{short-time-radial-compiler} of [S].  The audits show
no Magnus obstruction through order three for phase-type targets, a genuine
bounded-amplitude floor for population swaps outside the saturated-carrier
mechanism, a static-mask no-go for drift renormalization on rotation-complete
frames, and a forced parity-resolved leakage law.  The problem controls the
speed of remote-shell insertion physics, not the disproof above.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Effective constants]
Make $M_j$, the depths, and the radii effective along the trajectory
(atomicity of generated bundles plus quantitative Poisson barriers give
$M_j=O(\log j)$ under a resolved-block reading).  This would upgrade the
existence argument to a constructive one.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Other dimensions]
Carry the architecture to $X_\nu$ for $\nu\neq3$ and to the threshold
$\nu=2$, where the exterior theory differs most.
\end{problem}
